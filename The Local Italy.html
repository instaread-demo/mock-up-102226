EES and ETA: What to expect when travelling to and from Italy this Christmas

Emma Pearson - emma.pearson@thelocal.com
Published: 8 Dec, 2025 CET.Updated: Wed 24 Dec 2025 15:09 CET

Christmas is always a busy time for travel to and from Italy, but this year travellers may have to reckon with new rules, including the EU's Entry & Exit System and the UK's ETA.

If you haven’t crossed an international border for a few months, you might notice a few differences in travel this Christmas.

Over the past year, the EU has introduced its long-awaited and much-delayed Entry & Exit System (EES); meanwhile, the UK has extended its Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) to include nationals of EU countries such as Italy.

However, exactly how these will affect you depends on your nationality and residency status, as well as where you travel from and how you travel.

Here’s a quick preview of what to expect.

EES

Where – This affects anyone crossing an external EU/Schengen zone border (for example, travelling between Italy and the US, UK, Canada, and Australia). 

It does not, however, affect trips within the EU/Schengen zone, such as travel between Italy and France, Austria or Switzerland.

Who – The EES does not affect people who are travelling on the passport of any EU/EEA country. Also exempt are people who are legally resident in an EU/EEA country – so, for example, an American living in Italy with a permesso di soggiorno.

READ ALSO: Travel to Italy: Your questions answered about EES and ETIAS

How – If you are not in one of the exempt groups and you have not crossed an EU external border since the EES was launched in October, you may have to complete EES pre-registration. 

Advertisement

This is done upon arrival at the relevant port/airport – you will be directed to the appropriate area and will have to fill out a questionnaire, as well as provide fingerprints and a facial scan.

The exact process may vary depending on the type of travel. 

At airports, passengers are generally directed to self-service kiosks to complete pre-registration checks.

Phased start – You’ll notice we said passengers may have to complete the formalities. Due to fears of long queues at peak times, the EU has allowed for a phased start.

That means that this Christmas, some airports will be operating EES, while others won’t.

Meanwhile, other terminals will be operating it for some travellers and not for others.

READ ALSO: LISTED: The big changes for travel to and from Italy in 2026

Advertisement

During the six-month introduction period (so until April 10th, 2026), entry ports also have the option to suspend EES registration on busy travel days and revert to manual passport stamping.

Pre-planning – Unlike ETA and ETIAS, there is nothing that you need to do in advance of your trip regarding the EES, as registration is done at the entry port itself.

However, the new system may cause delays and queues, so it’s best to note the time your travel operator recommends you arrive at the airport/port.

ETA

Where – This is a UK system, so it only affects people travelling into the UK.

Who – The ETA is required for everyone apart from those who are travelling on either a UK or Irish passport. People who have a long-stay visa for the UK, or have residency in the UK, are also exempt. 

Advertisement

When it comes to people who have dual nationality with the UK, the situation is complicated – find full details here.

Non-Brits who are married to a UK national aren't exempt from ETA.

READ ALSO: Do I need an ETA visa for my Italian spouse or children?

How – if you are not in one of the exempt groups, you will have to apply for the ETA before your travel. This can only be done online and costs £16 – once you have the ETA, it lasts for two years, unless you get a new passport during that period.

There is no exemption for children, and if you are travelling in a group, each group member must have their own ETA, even babies.

Full details on how it works here.

Phased start – The ETA has been introduced gradually since 2023, extending to all non-EU nationals (apart from Brits) in January and then being rolled out to include EU/EEA passport holders (with the exception of Irish passports) in April 2025.

Advertisement

However, earlier this month, the UK government issued an update saying that from February 2026, people would be turned back at the border if they did not have an ETA. 

This would seem to imply that people are not actually being turned away at the moment, even though the ETA is officially mandatory, which would provide some breathing space over Christmas to dual nationals who do not have a current UK passport.

However, the advice remains to get the ETA if required.

Make sure you are using the official UK government website or app – there is a growing number of scam ‘ETA’ sites.

Pre-planning – The ETA must be applied for in advance of travel. The UK government says that most applications are processed ‘within minutes’, but the official advice is to apply online at least three days in advance of your trip – especially at busy times like Christmas.

If you’re going to the UK, remember that new Foot and Mouth regulations have restricted the import of meat and cheese from the EU – full details here.

Advertisement

And ETIAS?

This is the second phase of the EU’s travel changes, introducing a visa waiver for visitors and tourists coming into the EU/Schengen zone.

This is very similar to the UK’s ETA (and the US’s ESTA), but it’s not something that you need to worry about this Christmas – it is currently due to be introduced in the second half of 2026.
